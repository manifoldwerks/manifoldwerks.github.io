Glasswerk01

Glasswerk, in three steps.

1

Open a dataset. The first look starts on its own: a cell map and a lineage recipe.

2

Ask what your data can answer. Each question names the method and the paper behind it.

3

Every run ends with a figure, a one-line result and its caveats. Enough to call the next experiment.

NextBring a hypothesis. Glasswerk will look through every view of your cells, keep the ones that hold, and say why. When none holds, it says so, and says what would change that: a sharper question, or more cells. You check the biology you see, and only that.

The problem02

Today that question costs a week.

Dozens of 2D plots. One tool after another. The computational biologist who could tell you is booked until spring.

Glasswerk runs the workflow an expert would start with, on your own machine, and shows every step, setting and caveat. When a question needs a method it does not have, it says so instead of guessing.

How it works03

Your data stays in your lab.

Glasswerk runs on your machine. Nothing trains on your data. What travels is the method: the steps, settings and checks an expert would have chosen.

NextLabs that opt in will share which methods worked on data shaped like theirs. The data will never leave.

Open sourceThe engine is public: manyruns, manyagents and manylatents, by Latent Reasoning Works, on PyPI.

A motivating example04

Nature repeats her best ideas.

Starlings over a city and cells in a tissue sheet are described by the same family of equations. Methods written for one have been fitted to the other. Glasswerk starts from the same bet: a method proven on data shaped like yours is the place to begin on yours.

Sources: Vicsek et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 1995 · Toner and Tu, Phys. Rev. E 1998 · Szabó et al., Phys. Rev. E 2006 · Marchetti et al., Rev. Mod. Phys. 2013.
